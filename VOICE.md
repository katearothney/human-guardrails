# VOICE.md

Notes to myself so everything in this repo sounds like one person wrote it.

## What my writing does
First person, present tense, contractions. I say what I think and then say how sure I am.
I admit what I don't know early rather than burying it. "I'm not a neuroscientist and I don't have the answer" belongs near the top, not in a footnote.
Concrete over abstract. Alone time, moving your body, being bored, getting rest. Not "restorative practices."
Ordinary words. Stuff, thing, worse, sharp, costs us. Not leverage, robust, landscape, delve, unpack, dive into.
Sentences vary. Some run long because that's how thinking sounds. Not every paragraph is three beats.
Second person in the tables, first person in the prose.

## What I don't do
No em dashes. No semicolons.
No bolded labels repeated at the same spot in every section. If four sections all have a bolded "Why this matters," that's a template, not writing.
No fragment punchlines. "Not a bug. A feature." reads like a LinkedIn post.
No rule of three as a rhythm. Two examples is usually enough and three every time is a tell.
No evaluative asides about the research. "Underrated," "fascinating," "which I appreciate" sound like a reviewer. Just say the finding and say what I think about it in a full sentence.
No closing uplift. Sections can end on the actual last point.
No "it's not just X, it's Y."
No hedge stacking. Pick one. "Probably" or "seems," not "may potentially somewhat suggest."

## Structure
Every capacity page answers the same four things: what it is and why it's worth protecting, how AI wears it down, what the evidence says including the counter-evidence, and the guardrails split into the ones that have nothing to do with technology and the ones built into the technology.
Every claim gets an evidence grade. The grades are defined in the README and I use them consistently.
I keep the studies that argue against me. That's the whole point.

## Things to check before pushing
Read it out loud. If a sentence would be strange to say to a friend, rewrite it.
Search for the em dash character and the semicolon.
Count the bolded phrases. If there are more than a couple per page, cut them.
Check whether any paragraph could have been written by someone who hadn't read the paper. If yes, add the specific number or the specific finding.
