# Hypotheses

These are sharper than open questions. Each one is a claim I think might be true and that someone could actually test. For each, I list the claim, the worry behind it, where the research stands, and a rough idea of how you would test it.

Two tags: **[research happening]** means people are studying this now, and **[haven't seen research]** means I have not found good studies yet.

When a hypothesis gathers enough solid evidence, I move it into a full [capacity](../capacities/) page.

## 1. AI flattery narrows your mind
**Claim:** the more an assistant agrees with you and tells you how right you are, the less able you become to seriously consider views you disagree with.
**Worry behind it:** sycophancy plus echo chambers.
**Research:** [research happening], on sycophancy and filter bubbles. See [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md#epistemic-autonomy).
**How you would test it:** measure how well people can argue the other side of an issue after a stretch of using a very agreeable assistant versus a neutral one.

## 2. A curated feed shrinks your information diet
**Claim:** when the model chooses what you see, you get its slant and a narrower range of views than you would find on your own.
**Worry behind it:** model bias, and less exposure to a real range of views.
**Research:** [research happening], on algorithmic bias and filter bubbles.
**How you would test it:** compare the range of sources and viewpoints people meet through an AI assistant versus open search on the same questions.

## 3. Outsourcing care makes us less compassionate
**Claim:** if AI handles the hard conversations and some of the caring for us, we practice compassion less and get worse at it.
**Worry behind it:** compassion is a skill that needs practice.
**Research:** [haven't seen research]. Most of what exists is adjacent work on AI companions.
**How you would test it:** look at whether heavy use of AI for emotional or interpersonal tasks tracks with changes in real-world helping and empathy.

## 4. AI companionship reshapes how we relate to each other
**Claim:** as AI fills more social and emotional roles, it changes what we expect from real relationships, including dating and gender dynamics.
**Worry behind it:** easy AI company standing in for effortful human ties.
**Research:** [research happening], mostly through studies on AI companions and loneliness.
**How you would test it:** track relationship expectations and satisfaction among heavy versus light users of AI companions over time.

## 5. AI keeps us seated and indoors
**Claim:** the more we can do through a screen, the less we move and go out into the world, with costs to body and mind.
**Worry behind it:** convenience pulling us out of physical life.
**Research:** [research happening], but mostly adjacent: screen time and sedentary behavior, not AI specifically.
**How you would test it:** measure activity and time outdoors as people shift more of their tasks to AI.

## 6. Letting AI do the work erodes ownership
**Claim:** when AI produces our work, we understand it less, own it less, and are less able to stand behind what we put our name on.
**Worry behind it:** accountability for what we make and say.
**Research:** [haven't seen research]. This one feels wide open.
**How you would test it:** check whether people can accurately explain and defend work they made with heavy AI help versus work they made themselves.

## 7. Writing with AI erodes your own voice
**Claim:** the more you let AI write for you, the harder it gets to write and sound like yourself.
**Worry behind it:** I felt this while building this very project.
**Research:** [research happening], on AI making writing more similar across people.
**How you would test it:** compare how distinct and consistent someone's writing voice stays after months of writing with AI versus without.
